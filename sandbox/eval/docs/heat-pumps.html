How heat pmups work

A heat pump moves heat from one place to another. It does not burn fuel to make heat.

In winter, the outdoor unit takes heat from the cold air outside, even when the temperature is below zero, and a refrigerant carries that heat through a compressor, which raises its temperature a lot, and then the indoor unit releases the heat into the rooms of the house, where a fan or a set of radiators spreads it around so that every room gets warm.

In summer, the same machine runs the other way and cools the house.

What it costs to run

A heat pump uses electricity to move heat. For each unit of electricity, a good heat pump moves three to four units of heat into the house. Your bill depends on the price of electricity where you live and on how well the house keeps heat in.
